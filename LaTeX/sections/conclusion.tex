\section{Conclusion}
In this research, the effectiveness of an \acrshort{rnn} implemented for the RoboRIO system for classifying \acrshort{emg} signals
was examined. Looking back at the research questions, the structure for the \acrshort{rnn} model was constructed to facilitate RQ1
but the training was not functional. For RQ2 the method for driving the actuators at variable speeds was not implemented but could be
feasibly implemented as a subVI in Labview. Lastly, for RQ3 a full iteration of the \acrshort{rnn} in LabView took 230 ms, showing a
clear improvement compared to the testing of the exoskeleton made by the last group since it is now 4.3 times faster.

With the use of the Myoware sensors and Analog Discovery studio, the signal acquisition was processed without any large issues. 
This was due to the signals received from the sensors being consistent from user to user. 
The Analog Discovery studio studio made processing and transferring the signals to Python lightwork.

The research by Aviles et al. \cite{RNNEMG} showed the effectiveness of \acrshort{rnn} models for \acrshort{emg} signal classification and
the research by Zhou et al. \cite{shoulderexo} showed the effectiveness of using LabView for systems that require low latency which combined
with the work of \acrshort{afes} \cite{AFES} provides a system that combines the best of both worlds.

As mentioned in the discussion, the main limitation of the project was the lack of a working model. The \acrshort{rnn} model in Python was not
capable of outputting the four distinct states of function for the exoskeleton.

\subsection{Future Work}

PyTorch was used in this project which was found to be incompatible with LabView leading to difficulties when it comes to extracting the 
weights of the model, as well as the lack of a motor controller leading to physical tests of the model to not be possible. 
Any future projects should consider acquiring and setting up a motor controller to show visible feedback on the 
exoskeleton as well as solving the issues with the \acrshort{rnn} training as their main priorities.
An aspect to take into account in regards to the training of the \acrshort{rnn} is how the data is fed to the model. The issues
with the training could be caused by the repetitive nature of the data, where the same movement order is repeated. Splitting the data
into atomic movements and then combining them in a random order could be a solution to this issue.

Once the arm moves, two main directions of improvement could be considered. The first is to mount it on a person. After 
implementing and mounting it the second direction would be to add more DoF for example shoulder movement.

In the future if all these changes were applied this project would be viable to assist workers in that work using extended and heavy 
physically static positions.
